
MY AUTOBIOGRAPHY
CYRUS N. YOUNQUOI
From Humble Beginnings to a Life of Purpose, Education, and Service
My name is Cyrus N. Younquoi, and my story is a testament to resilience, determination, faith, family, and the transformative power of education.
My journey began on January 17, 1994, in Karnwee Town, Lessonnoh Chiefdom, Nimba County, Republic of Liberia, where I was born to Mr. Moses Younquoi and Mrs. Martha Borman Younquoi. I was the youngest of four children, growing up alongside two sisters and a brother. My childhood was shaped by the warmth of family, the simplicity of village life, and the challenges that would eventually strengthen my character and determination.
My formal education began in 1991 at Vokpor High School. At the age of seven, my life took an important turn when my cousin, Mr. Roosevelt Zarwee, and his wife, Mrs. Dee Zarwee, came to Karnwee and took me to Saclepea in search of better educational opportunities. I enrolled at Saclepea Red Cross High School, beginning a new chapter in my educational journey.
Unfortunately, Liberia's civil war disrupted my education and forced my family and me to move from one place to another. I continued my studies at Kpallah Inland Mission, where, despite the uncertainty surrounding us, I remained committed to learning. I was promoted to the third grade, but continued instability eventually took me to Tiapa, where I lived with my late uncle, Mr. Robert Yeanay. In 1996, he continued to support my education and encouraged me to remain focused despite the difficult circumstances.
I later returned to Karnwee and subsequently moved back to Saclepea, where I lived with my late aunt, Mother Theresa Daysee. In 2001, while in the seventh grade, I continued my education at Johnny Voker High School. I remained there until I was promoted to the tenth grade, after which I moved to Ganta City.
In Ganta, I attended J. W. Pearson High School beginning in 2004. After progressing to the twelfth grade, I made another major decision: I moved to Monrovia to continue my education. There, I attended night school at New Destiny Christian High School, which at the time was known as J. Chauncey Goodridge Jr. Memorial High School.
Balancing education with life's many challenges was not easy. Yet, I refused to allow my circumstances to define my future. Through perseverance and determination, I graduated with high honors.
My pursuit of education did not end with secondary school. In 2014, I began my higher education journey at St. Clements University, where I pursued a Bachelor of Business Administration (B.B.A.) in Human Resource Management and graduated with high honors in 2021.
That same year, an opportunity opened the door to another significant chapter of my academic journey. Through a scholarship, I traveled to Türkiye to pursue a master's degree at Cyprus International University, where I earned an MSc in Healthcare Organization Management, graduating with high honors.
Behind this important milestone was the invaluable support and guidance of Dr. Julie Taylor, whose encouragement, mentorship, and unwavering belief in my potential played a significant role in my pursuit of higher education. She was not merely a supporter of my academic journey; she became one of the people who helped open doors that transformed the direction of my life.
I am deeply grateful to Dr. Taylor for recognizing potential in me, encouraging me to pursue greater opportunities, and standing behind my educational aspirations. Her contribution to my academic development is a meaningful part of my story, and I will always acknowledge her as one of the important individuals who helped shape my journey toward higher education and professional growth.
While pursuing my master's degree, I continued expanding my knowledge through additional professional studies. In 2022, I earned an Advanced Diploma in Entrepreneurship, Leadership, and Management from Tazama Leadership School of Pennsylvania, USA. I also earned a Certificate in Customer Relationship Management (CRM) from Kingston Business School in Manchester, United Kingdom.
My academic journey eventually led me to doctoral studies. I became a PhD candidate in Public Health, with a focus on Environmental Health, at the University of Port Harcourt's World Bank Africa Centre of Excellence, Centre for Public Health and Toxicological Research (ACE-PUTOR). Through my doctoral research, I seek to contribute to a deeper understanding of environmental and public health challenges and to help develop solutions that can improve the health and well-being of communities. 
My professional journey has been equally diverse. 
From September 2008 to August 2014, I served as a classroom teacher at the Betty Memorial Institute in Grand Cape Mount County, Liberia. Teaching gave me the opportunity to educate, mentor, and inspire young people while developing my own skills in planning, communication, leadership, and critical thinking.
I later transitioned into administration and served as an Assistant Admissions Advisor at Tazama Leadership School of Pennsylvania, USA, from March 2022 to January 2024. In that position, I supported students, managed administrative responsibilities, and contributed to the effective operation of the institution.
My professional experience also includes serving as a Data Analyst at Refuge Place International (RPI), where I have applied analytical, administrative, and leadership skills to support impactful initiatives, particularly within the healthcare and development sectors.
I also serve as the hospital Administrator at the Esther & Jereline Medical Center in Ganta, the second biggest government hospital in Nimba County Republic of Liberia.
Beyond my professional career, I have embraced entrepreneurship. I am the Founder and CEO of Blue Lake Group of Companies, an enterprise that represents my entrepreneurial vision, commitment to innovation, and desire to create opportunities for others.
Looking back, I recognize that my journey was never a straight road. It was a journey filled with uncertainty, displacement, financial limitations, educational interruptions, sacrifices, and moments when giving up could have seemed easier than continuing.
But every challenge became part of my preparation.
From the town of Karnwee to Saclepea, Kpallah, Tiapa, Ganta, and Monrovia; from the classroom to university lecture halls; from Liberia to Türkiye, Europe, and the United States; and from teaching and administration to public health, research, data analysis, Hospital Administrator, and entrepreneurship, each chapter has contributed to the person I am today.
I remain deeply grateful to the people who believed in me when my circumstances did not necessarily suggest what my future could become. I especially remember my late parents, Mr. Moses Younquoi and Mrs. Martha Borman Younquoi, my late aunt Mother Theresa Daysee, my late uncle Mr. Robert Yeanay, and my cousin Mr. Roosevelt Zarwee and his wife, Mrs. Dee Zarwee and Dr. Julie Taylor Their support, sacrifices, encouragement, and belief in my potential helped shape my journey.
Today, I am legally married and blessed with a beautiful family and several children. My family represents another important chapter of my life and reminds me that success is not only about personal achievement but also about building a legacy for those who come after us.
My story is not simply about academic degrees, professional positions, or entrepreneurial accomplishments. It is about where I came from, what I endured, the people who helped me along the way, and the determination that kept me moving forward.
I began my journey with very little, but I carried something powerful with me: hope.
I learned that circumstances may delay you, but they do not have to define you. I learned that education can open doors that once seemed impossible to reach. I learned that resilience is built through adversity, and that every difficult chapter can become preparation for a greater purpose.
From humble beginnings in Karnwee to a life dedicated to education, public health, leadership, service, family, and entrepreneurship, my journey continues.
This is my story.
